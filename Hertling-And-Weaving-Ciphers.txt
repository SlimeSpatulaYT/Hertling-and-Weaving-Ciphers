The Hertling Cipher and Weaving Cipher are two monoalphabetic substitution ciphers based on rearranging the standard 26-letter English alphabet.

The Hertling Cipher uses an alphabetic sequence formed by alternating letters from the end and beginning of the alphabet:

ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ
ZAYBXCWDVEUFTGSHRIQJPKOLNM

This means A becomes Z, B becomes A, C becomes Y, D becomes B, and so on. The substitution alphabet can be understood as interleaving the normal alphabet with the reversed alphabet.

The Weaving Cipher instead interleaves the two halves of the alphabet:

ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ
NAOBPCQDRESFTGUHVIWJXKYLZM

Thus A becomes N, B becomes A, C becomes O, D becomes B, E becomes P, and so on. In other words, the first half of the alphabet (A–M) and the second half (N–Z) are woven together letter by letter.

Both are deterministic monoalphabetic substitution systems, meaning that each plaintext letter is consistently replaced by one corresponding ciphertext letter. The names "Hertling Cipher" and "Weaving Cipher" are proposed names for these specific substitution patterns.

For example, if you wanted to encode "I like Five Nights at Freddy's" using either cipher, it would look like this:

Plaintext:
I like Five Nights at Freddy's

Hertling Cipher:
V fvux Cvkx Gvwdjq zj Cixbbn'q

Weaving Cipher:
R frsp Crkp Grqdjw nj Cipbbz'w

Capitalization, spaces, and symbols are preserved. Only alphabetic letters are substituted.

The Hertling Cipher and Weaving Cipher were independently devised by the
author. Because these are simple alphabetic constructions, similar or
identical substitution patterns may have been devised previously. No claim
is made regarding priority of invention; this document establishes the
definitions and terminology used for these ciphers here.
